Frequently Asked Questions

Will bankruptcy ruin my credit forever?
No. Chapter 7 stays on your credit report for 10 years and Chapter 13 for 7 years — but the impact fades significantly over time, especially as you rebuild. Many people qualify for credit cards within 1–2 years and FHA mortgages within 2 years of discharge.
Do I need a lawyer to file bankruptcy?
You are legally allowed to file "pro se" (without an attorney), and for simple Chapter 7 cases tools like Upsolve.org can help. However, Chapter 13 and 11 cases are complex and an attorney is strongly recommended. Many bankruptcy attorneys offer free consultations and accept payment plans.
Can I keep my house and car?
In Chapter 7, you can keep secured property if you are current on payments and the equity is within your state's exemption limits. In Chapter 13, you can catch up on mortgage arrears and keep your home even if you're behind — this is one of Chapter 13's biggest advantages.
What debts cannot be discharged?
Most student loans, child support, alimony, recent tax debts, criminal fines, and debts from fraud are generally non-dischargeable. Some older income taxes may be dischargeable — ask your attorney.
How does the Means Test work?
The Means Test compares your average monthly income over the past 6 months to your state's median income for your household size. If you're below the median, you generally qualify for Chapter 7. If above, your disposable income (after allowed expenses) is calculated — if it's low enough, you may still qualify.
Is this tool a substitute for legal advice?
No. This tool provides educational information only and cannot account for all variables in your situation. Bankruptcy law is complex and state-specific. Always consult a licensed bankruptcy attorney before making any filing decisions.
